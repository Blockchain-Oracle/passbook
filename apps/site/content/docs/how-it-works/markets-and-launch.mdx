---
title: Markets and Launch
description: Two bearer-position venues, with different pricing, creation and settlement paths.
---

Markets and Launch share one privacy pattern: shielded value leaves the STRK20 pool for a venue,
and the venue records a **commitment** instead of an owner address. The browser keeps the secret
behind that commitment so the position can be cashed out, claimed, redeemed or refunded later.

That shared shape does not make them the same product. Markets settles a priced side against
Pragma. Launch sells fixed units along an epoch staircase and ends in either a deployed token or a
full refund. The contracts are deployed on Starknet mainnet; addresses and deploy transactions are
in [the evidence](/docs/evidence).

<Callout type="warn" title="Bearer means the secret controls the position">
The browser stores each position secret before it broadcasts the transaction. Keep the recovery
backup while a position remains open. A commitment can be read publicly, but it cannot claim the
position without its secret.
</Callout>

### 1. The board is a live read surface

The client reads the current and previous window for every active series, recent custom markets,
the idle house float, contract quotes and the public event tape. Pragma prices feed the cards and
charts. TanStack Query owns those reads; the chain feed writes into the same cache, with RPC polling
as the fallback.

An untouched standing window is not yet a stored market. Its id and deadline are derived from the
series and the current epoch. The first valid bet opens it, takes a fresh Pragma median as the line
and moves that series' seed out of the house float. A bet too late in the window, below the opening
minimum, or against a stale or thin oracle is refused before any market opens.

### 2. A bet creates a bearer claim

The ticket asks the Markets contract for `quote_bet`, then the browser creates a random secret and
stores `poseidon(secret)` as the public commitment. The secret is written to the local position
record before submission. The pool spends shielded STRK and calls `privacy_invoke(OP_BET, …)` in the
same transaction.

The market uses a constant-product reserve machine. A stake buys tickets; a winning ticket pays one
unit of the stake token. Standing series deduct their displayed vig before pricing the tickets.
Every rounding decision goes toward the pot so the contract cannot promise more than it holds.

### 3. The record is public, but has no bettor field

`BetPlaced` publishes the market id, side, amount, tickets, reserve movement and commitment. The
transaction submitter and timing are public too. If a covered transaction is used, the relayer is
the submitter and also learns network metadata; otherwise the user's account submits and pays.

The commitment is not an address field. That protects the bettor's identity only when the public
facts do not single the position out. A unique amount or an otherwise unique pattern can identify a
bet, which is why the app says the denomination needs company.

### 4. Cash-out, resolve and void are separate doors

Before the deadline, a bettor can sell the **whole** position back to the machine. The quote moves
with the reserves, and the transaction carries a 1% tolerance floor so a worse landing price
reverts.

After the deadline, anyone may call `resolve` during the five-minute resolution window. The
contract reads Pragma, checks the price scale and freshness, and for standing markets also checks
the minimum source count. Strictly above the line wins Over; equal to or below wins Under.

If no valid resolution lands, anyone may call `void` after ten minutes. Bettors then reclaim what
they paid. A resolved winner claims tickets; a custom-market seeder claims the residual. Each
terminal payout is pulled back through the pool into a fresh open note.

## Launch architecture

### 1. Creation is deliberately a public path

Creating a launch moves no sale money, so it calls `create_launch` directly instead of going through
`privacy_invoke`. The transaction's submitting account is public: normally the creator's account,
or the relayer when the creator explicitly spends a covered transaction.

Before submission, the browser stores a creator secret and sends only its commitment. That secret
controls the later sweep of the raise; the transaction submitter does not. An optional logo is
pinned before the chain call when pinning is configured. The contract checks the URI length but
does not fetch or endorse whatever the URI serves.

### 2. The curve advances in whole units

Every epoch contains exactly 16 units. All units inside one epoch have the same price; the unit
price is `p0 + epoch × step`. A purchase that crosses an epoch boundary is split across the curve,
so its later units pay the later price. Prices are per unit rather than per token, avoiding a
fractional token price that would round to zero in integer contract arithmetic.

The buyer reads `quote_buy`, chooses a whole-unit count, and reviews the shielded STRK cost. The
browser stores a new buyer secret, then the pool sends the stake to
`privacy_invoke(OP_BUY, …)`. `Bought` publishes the launch, starting epoch, units, total cost,
post-buy sold count and commitment. It has no buyer-address field, but the amount, timing and
transaction submitter remain public.

### 3. Sold out and missed deadline are different state machines

When every unit sells, **anyone** may call `graduate`. The Launch contract deploys a token from its
pinned class hash, mints the complete supply to itself and books that supply for buyer redemption.
Each buyer reveals the position secret through a pool-only redeem operation, and the launch tokens
land as a new pool note.

The creator uses the separate creator secret to sweep the raised STRK to a named public address.
That recipient and amount are public. Graduation does not create a DEX market, add liquidity, pay a
royalty or offer a buy-back.

If the deadline passes before the units sell out, the first valid refund marks the launch failed.
No keeper is required. Each buyer then reclaims exactly that position's recorded cost into a new
STRK pool note.

### 4. Talk is not part of token settlement

Each launch page also exposes an open Talk thread. It uses the same relay family as Chat, keeps at
most 50 messages in relay memory and is readable by anyone who derives the room from the launch id.
It does not write to the Launch contract and does not affect a buy, graduation, redeem, sweep or
refund.

## Markets and Launch side by side

| Boundary | Markets | Launch |
|---|---|---|
| Free reads | Market registry, events, float, Pragma price and quotes | Launch registry, metadata, curve state and quotes |
| Opening action | Standing window opens on its first valid bet; custom markets use the pool | Creator calls `create_launch` directly |
| Buyer input | Shielded STRK through the pool | Shielded STRK through the pool |
| Public position facts | Side, amount, tickets, odds movement, timing and commitment | Units, cost, epoch, sold count, timing and commitment |
| Local bearer material | Bet or seed secret | Buyer secret and, for creators, a separate sweep secret |
| Early exit | Whole-position cash-out before close | No sell-back before graduation |
| Success path | Pragma resolution, then winning-ticket or seeder-residual claim | Token deployment, buyer redemption, public creator sweep |
| Failure path | Permissionless void, then cash-in refund | First refund marks failed, then cash-in refund |
| Returned value | Stake-token pool note | Launch-token pool note on success; stake-token note on failure |
