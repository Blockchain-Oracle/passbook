Chat is the free one. Multiple conversations, sealed messages, and value attached to a message so
that paying someone and telling them why are one action — and sending an ordinary message costs
nothing, because it is not a Starknet transaction at all.

That last clause is the whole trade. [Mail](/docs/how-it-works/mail) puts a sealed memo on chain
inside a proved pool transaction and charges the pool fee for it. Chat hands ciphertext to our
relay and charges nothing, and in exchange nothing about it is on chain and nothing is durable.
Both surfaces ship, because they are answers to different questions.

## Chat and Mail, side by side

| | **Chat** (`/chat`) | **Mail** (`/mail`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost to send** | Nothing. Not a transaction. | The live pool fee plus gas, unless a covered transaction applies. |
| **On chain?** | No. Nothing is written. | Yes — the pool posts the sealed memo to our Mailbox in the same proved transaction as the payment. |
| **Transport** | Our relay carries the ciphertext over one multiplexed stream. | No message relay. The recipient reads the Mailbox event from Starknet. |
| **Persistence** | Up to 50 ciphertext envelopes per relay room for 30 idle minutes, plus this browser's bounded local log. | Chain-persistent. The ciphertext is a Mailbox event. |
| **Recovery on a new device** | Not recovered. A cleared browser loses its history. | Rebuilt from the chain and the viewing key alone. |
| **What the relay learns** | IP, timing, sizes, the sender key hint, and which rooms share one client connection. It can correlate the participants conservatively described by the app's disclosure. | Only the transaction request and IP/timing, and only when it submits a covered transaction. |
| **Money** | Optional attachment; the payment is a separate fee-bearing pool transaction. | Required: a chosen amount or 0.01-token postage and the memo settle atomically. |

**Attaching money is a transaction either way.** "Gas-free" describes sending an ordinary chat
message. A payment sent from inside a room is an ordinary pool transaction with an ordinary fee —
the message rides the relay, the money does not.

## The honest parts

**The auditor is inside the trust boundary.** Room keys derive from the same viewing keys that are
[escrowed to the auditor](/docs/privacy/the-auditor) at registration, so the auditor can derive the
room secret and read the messages.

**It is not on-chain, decentralized, or persistent either.** Those words get used loosely about
messaging and none of them is true here. One process holds the backlog, in memory, and if it
restarts the backlog is gone.

**One socket, disclosed.** Every conversation rides a single multiplexed connection. That means a
multiplexed subscribe tells the relayer that those rooms share one participant. The app's own copy
says so rather than implying otherwise — the alternative, a socket per room, has its own timing
tells and costs more.

**The relayer holds a short backlog.** Ciphertext only, in memory, bounded, dropped after a quiet
period. It holds no key of yours.

The exact bounds are 50 envelopes per room and 30 idle minutes. They help a reconnect; they are not
a mailbox and cannot fill a gap after the browser has been closed longer than that.

## The name directory

Claiming a name publishes `name → address` on-chain. That is its entire function — a directory
nobody can read is not a directory — and it is opt-in.

**Search, however, is private.** The client fetches the whole list and matches locally, so the
relayer never learns who you looked for. That asymmetry is deliberate: publishing your own name is
your choice, and looking someone up should not be a disclosure.
